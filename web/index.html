About Fly Hero

What this is

A model of a fruit fly's brain, playing a Clone Hero–style chart. The wiring is real: all 139,241 neurons of the adult Drosophila brain and the 2,700,429 connections between them, taken from the FlyWire v783 connectome and never altered. Each neuron is simulated as a rate — roughly, how hard it is driving its targets — not as individual spikes. The only things training changed are each cell type's time constant and bias, one gain per type-pair, and the linear readout that turns descending-neuron activity into button presses.

Everything here is a recording. The fly played these songs once; the site replays what happened, frame by frame. Nothing trains, learns or decides while you watch, and scrubbing moves every panel to the same instant of the same take.

What you are looking at

  • Through the fly's eyes is the fly's actual input — the contrast arriving at 11,118 photoreceptors, which is all the brain ever receives. The note chart, the lane colors and the note timings are not fed in anywhere else. The CRT is for you, not for the fly.
  • Connectome shows the recorded neurons flashing at their own activity, over the whole brain drawn at real soma positions. 4,096 of the 139,241 were recorded individually (every descending neuron, a photoreceptor sample, and the most variable of the rest); the active count is out of those 4,096, and the mean activation is over all 139,241.
  • Descending neurons are the six numbers the brain actually outputs. The tick is the decoder's 0.50 threshold; a bar lights when that output really fired, read from the same recording the CRT's fret lights come from. Strum is deliberately not just "past the tick": it fires on a peak above it, so a high strum bar with no strum is the decoder waiting for the maximum.
  • Rate oscilloscope is the mean rate of every excitatory neuron (83,793) and every inhibitory one (45,550), over the 20 seconds up to the playhead.
  • The CRT is a presentation, not a measurement. The bezel, the scanlines, the lane colors and the perspective are drawn for you. What is recorded is which frets were held on each frame and when a note counted as hit, missed or overstrummed — that is what the lit frets and the flashes come from. Only the connectome, eyes, decision and oscilloscope panels show the model's own signals.

How well it actually plays

On Medium charts from songs it never trained on, it hits 0.699 of notes (mean over 29 held-out songs, standard error 0.019) and overstrums about 40 times a minute. We set 0.70 as the bar before running it, so that is a narrow miss, reported as it came out.

All three songs on this site are songs it practiced. They were picked to be watchable, and they flatter it: 0.89, 0.88 and 0.91. The honest number for a song it has never seen is the 0.699 above.

Limitations

  • A rate model, not a spiking one: no action potentials, no dendrites, no synaptic delays beyond a single time constant per cell type.
  • No neuromodulation and no gap junctions. Every connection is chemical, and its sign is FlyWire's predicted transmitter — 23,387 neurons fall back to a cell-type consensus and 919 to a default, so the excitatory/inhibitory split on the oscilloscope is a prediction, not a measurement.
  • The readout is learned, not anatomical. Real flies have no wiring from descending neurons to a guitar controller; that map is fitted.
  • A simulated game, not Clone Hero itself: our own renderer and our own scoring, hammer-ons/pull-offs and taps treated as ordinary strums, open notes removed.
  • 21,141 of the 139,241 neurons (15%) are drawn at an anchor point rather than a true soma, and are dimmed to show it.
  • It was trained by copying a scripted expert player, not by discovering how to play on its own.
